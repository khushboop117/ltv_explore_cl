How this is calculated

LTV here is expected cash collected per new subscriber within the horizon: price × probability of making each payment, summed over the payments that fall inside the window.

1. Turn each subscription into a payment count

Each subscription is converted into the number of charges it paid. The end dates in the file follow exact rules, which the conversion reproduces for every one of the 57,739 ended subscriptions:

  • A billing period is 365 days (annual) or 365.25 ÷ 12 ≈ 30.44 days (monthly). Renewal k is charged on day round(k × period).
  • Voluntary cancels end access on the next renewal date, so the last period is paid in full.
  • Payment failures end access exactly 28 days after the failed renewal (the retry window). The failed charge is not revenue.

2. Estimate the chance of each renewal

For every renewal (2nd payment, 3rd payment, …) the tool counts how many subscribers reached it and how many churned there. That gives an observed survival curve, built Kaplan–Meier style so younger cohorts still contribute to early renewals.

A renewal’s outcome is only counted once renewal date + 28 days has passed by 30 Jun 2026. Otherwise a failing card still in retries would look like a payer, and voluntary churn (visible on the renewal date) would be counted before payment failures (visible 28 days later). Pending cancels are treated the same way, so nothing about the future leaks in.

3. Extend the curve past the data

Data runs out at 36 monthly payments and 3 annual payments. Past the point where fewer than 100 subscribers are still at risk, the curve continues with a fitted model. Voluntary churn and payment failure are each modeled as a shifted‑beta‑geometric process (a standard subscription model where churn falls over time as less loyal subscribers leave first), and combined as competing risks.

Does the tail model hold up?

Backtest on monthly plans: fit only on the first 12 renewals, then predict the next two years and compare with what actually happened.

Simpler options missed in opposite directions. One beta‑geometric curve for all churn predicted 41.2% (too optimistic, because payment failures don’t fade the way voluntary churn does). A flat payment‑failure rate predicted 33.6% (too pessimistic). Modeling both separately lands closest.

Assumptions

  • Prices are fixed at $15/month and $150/year. No refunds, taxes, promotions or plan switches.
  • A cohort window is pooled into one curve. Early renewal rates have been stable since mid‑2023, so pooling is reasonable, but narrow the window if you suspect a change.
  • Annual plans have only two observed renewals, so their tail past year 3 rests on the model. Read 48–60 month annual LTVs as estimates.
  • Segments with fewer than 200 at‑risk renewals borrow the model shape from the full selection and are flagged “thin”.
  • LTV is revenue, not profit, unless you lower “Revenue kept”. Acquisition cost is never subtracted inside LTV.

Using this for paid marketing

  • Set bids per source from max CPA = LTV ÷ target ratio at the payback horizon finance accepts, instead of one blended CPA.
  • Watch the first‑renewal chart weekly. It moves within two months of a campaign change, long before LTV can.
  • Send value back to ad platforms: post a predicted‑LTV conversion value by plan and source, so bidding optimizes for subscribers who stay.
  • Push annual plans where they’re weak. An annual signup is worth more than a monthly one from the same source.
  • About one cancellation in five is a failed payment, not a choice. Better card retries and updater services raise LTV for every channel.
